Modulation

An LFO is a slow oscillator, a circle turning in time with the music. Here it moves the shapes: rotation, rounding, stretch, the hub, the circles' radii, the filter or the size. Since the shape is the sound, the timbre moves with it. Drag outward in an LFO for depth and around for rate.

Keyboard

Play the keys above or your computer keyboard (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K, Z and X change octave). They play the selected oscillator. Connect a MIDI keyboard to play all three.

Tone wheel

The wheel is the scale. Tap a note to add it or take it out. Drag around to transpose: one step is a semitone in chromatic order and a fifth in fifths order. Mode keeps the root and rotates the pattern of steps, so major becomes Dorian, Phrygian and so on.

XY scope

The left channel is drawn as x and the right as y. Switch an oscillator to XY and the sound draws its own shape, as in oscilloscope music.

Factory

Harmonices is a synthesizer and sequencer about the links between geometry and sound. Every sound is a shape, and every rhythm is a figure.

The circle is the sine wave

Let a point go round a circle and follow only its height over time. You get a sine wave, and that is the definition of sine. The wave behind every shape in Sound is exactly that height.

The shape sets the timbre

A diamond traced at constant speed gives an exact triangle wave. A square gives a trapezoid wave, close to a square wave but softer, because the point never jumps. Rotate the square 45° and it becomes a diamond and the wave a triangle. Rotating a shape changes its overtones, except for the circle.

Eccentricity makes a saw

Pull the hub off centre and let the angle advance evenly. The point races past on the near side and crawls on the far side. The wave leans and approaches a sawtooth. The same idea is called phase distortion and was used in Casio's CZ synths.

Circles on circles are additive synthesis

A circle riding on another circle draws spirograph patterns. The height of the outer point is a sum of sine waves whose frequencies have the same ratios as the circles' speeds. Fourier showed that every periodic wave can be built this way. Whole numbers give figures that close and tones that sound harmonic.

FM is a circle being pushed

In FM the modulator changes the speed of the carrier's circle, not its radius. That is why FM is drawn as a flower: the sound wound around the fundamental's turn. Ratios 1:2 and 1:3 give still flowers and harmonic tones. The ratio 1:φ never closes and sounds like bells and metal.

Rhythm and pitch are the same thing

The polyrhythm 3:4:5 is three pulses in one cycle. Play it about a hundred times faster and it becomes three tones with frequencies 3:4:5, a major chord in second inversion. Try the button in Polygons mode.

Fibonacci and the intervals

The ratios between neighbours in Fibonacci, 2/1, 3/2, 5/3, 8/5, 13/8, are the octave, fifth, major sixth and minor sixth. They approach φ ≈ 1.618, about 833 cents, between the two sixths. Fibonacci modulo m always repeats after the Pisano period, so the sequence becomes a loop.

The golden angle

137.5° is the turn that is hardest to approximate with fractions. The seeds never land in the same place yet spread evenly. Drag the angle away from it and watch the sunflower fall apart into arms: 120° gives three, 90° gives four.

Echo and room

An echo is the same wave coming back after a set time, weaker each round, so every echo sends a copy of the shape outward, in time. A room is thousands of tiny echoes melting into a tail, shown here as glow that shines brighter and lingers longer.

The filter shapes the shape

A low-pass filter removes high overtones, and the overtones are what make corners sharp. Close the low-pass and the square rounds off until only the circle, the fundamental, is left. The dashed outline is the original shape and the glowing line is what the filter lets through.

Scales as polygons

In the tone wheel the whole-tone scale is a hexagon, an augmented chord a triangle and a diminished seventh a square. Twelve pure fifths are almost seven octaves. The gap, the Pythagorean comma of 23.5 cents, is why the tunings sound different.

The octave of light

Visible light spans about one octave, 400 to 790 THz. Raise A 440 Hz by forty octaves and you land at 484 THz, about 620 nm, which is orange. The note colours are built on that sum. It is a play with numbers: eyes and ears work differently and no note has a real colour. Newton tied seven colours to seven notes and Scriabin coloured the circle of fifths.

Shortcuts and setup

Space plays and stops. Ctrl or Cmd + S saves the patch. A W S E D F T G Y H U J K play notes, Z and X change octave. MIDI, Bluetooth MIDI and Ableton Link work in the standalone version served by the small bridge in the project (see README). There you can also export recordings as WAV.

Sound is made in the browser with Web Audio. Shape waves are turned into overtone spectra (FFT) so they stay clean in the treble.
